# Presenting trade-offs instead of absorbing or refusing

Reached from question 4.

## The reframe

"Learn to say no" is common advice and hard to act on, because refusing a stakeholder without authority mostly reads as obstruction.

The version that works: **engineers do not say no, they present what it takes and surface the trade-offs.**

The shape:

> Yes. Here is what is currently in flight, here is what this costs, which do you want prioritized?

The goal is guiding the outcome and leaving the requester feeling heard, not blocking. It also moves the prioritization decision to the person who actually owns it, which is where it belonged.

## Why this is easier than it sounds

Refusing requires standing on conviction alone. Presenting a cost requires only knowing what is in flight and what things cost, which is information you already have and the requester does not.

The asymmetry is the whole point. The requester is not being unreasonable, they cannot see the queue.

## The form

Short, specific, and ending in a real question:

- **What is in flight**, named, with the commitment it belongs to.
- **What this costs**, in the same units. Cycle capacity, a delayed launch, a specific thing that slips.
- **The choice**, stated as a choice. Two or three options with their consequences.
- **A recommendation**, because handing over an unweighted menu is its own kind of abdication.

## Failure modes

**The wall of caveats.** A long list of risks and conditions reads as reluctance dressed up as analysis. Short is more credible.

**The fake choice.** If there is no real option being offered, it is a refusal wearing a costume and everyone can tell. Either find a genuine second option or say no plainly.

**No recommendation.** You have more context than the requester. Withholding your read to appear neutral wastes it.

## Apply it to your own plate

The same framing applies inward, and this is the part that gets missed.

Enablement work is still work. A quarter that hands you a proposal to write, an architecture to influence, a project to delegate, a forum to hand off, and an on-call rotation to build is a full quarter, even though none of it looks like shipping. Taking all of it on personally while being told to do less personally is the same overcommitment pattern in a new costume.

Run question 4 on yourself before accepting the next thing.
